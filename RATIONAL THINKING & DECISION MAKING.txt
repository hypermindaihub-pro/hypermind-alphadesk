RATIONAL THINKING & DECISION MAKING


# **Page 1: Understanding Rational Thinking**

## **1. What is Rational Thinking?**

Rational thinking means using facts, evidence, and careful reasoning to make decisions instead of relying only on emotions, guesses, or peer pressure.

A rational thinker asks:

* What are the facts?
* What evidence do I have?
* What are the possible consequences?
* Which choice makes the most sense?

Rational thinking helps us make wise decisions in school, at home, and in everyday life.

---

## **2. Difference Between Emotional and Rational Thinking**

### Emotional Thinking

Making decisions based mainly on feelings.

Example:

> "I'm angry, so I'll stop being friends with him."

---

### Rational Thinking

Making decisions based on facts and careful consideration.

Example:

> "I'm angry now, but I should first find out why he behaved that way before ending the friendship."

---

## **Real-Life Example 1: Exam Preparation**

Two students have an exam tomorrow.

### Student A

Thinks:

> "I'll play games all evening and study later."

He studies for only 20 minutes.

---

### Student B

Thinks:

> "The exam is important. If I study now, I will be better prepared."

He studies for two hours.

---

Who is thinking more rationally?

**Student B**, because he considered the future consequences of his decision.

---

## **3. Why Rational Thinking is Important**

Rational thinking helps students:

* Make better decisions
* Solve problems effectively
* Avoid mistakes
* Resist peer pressure
* Achieve their goals
* Become responsible individuals

---

## **4. Understanding Consequences**

Every decision has consequences.

A consequence is what happens after we make a choice.

### Example

Choice:

> Not doing homework.

Possible Consequences:

* Poor grades
* Teacher's correction
* Difficulty understanding lessons

Rational thinkers consider consequences before acting.

# **Page 2: Making Better Decisions**

## **5. Steps for Good Decision Making**

### Step 1: Identify the Problem

Ask:

> "What decision do I need to make?"

---

### Step 2: Gather Facts

Ask:

> "What information do I know?"

---

### Step 3: Consider Different Options

Ask:

> "What choices do I have?"

---

### Step 4: Think About Consequences

Ask:

> "What might happen if I choose this option?"

---

### Step 5: Choose the Best Option

Select the choice with the most positive outcome.

---

## **Real-Life Example 2: Spending Pocket Money**

A student receives ₦5,000.

He has two options:

### Option A

Spend all the money on snacks today.

### Option B

Use ₦2,000 for snacks and save ₦3,000 for school supplies.

A rational thinker would consider future needs and choose Option B.

---

## **6. Avoiding Peer Pressure**

Peer pressure happens when friends try to influence your decisions.

### Example

A group of students says:

> "Let's skip class. Everyone is doing it."

A rational thinker asks:

* Is this the right thing to do?
* What could happen if I get caught?
* Will this affect my learning?

Instead of following the crowd, they make their own decision.

---

## **Real-Life Example 3: Online Information**

A student sees a social media post:

> "Drinking a special drink will make you smarter in one day."

Before believing it, a rational thinker asks:

* Who posted this?
* Is there scientific evidence?
* Is the source trustworthy?

This helps prevent being misled by false information.

---

## **7. Good Habits of Rational Thinkers**

✔ They ask questions.

✔ They look for evidence.

✔ They think before acting.

✔ They consider consequences.

✔ They learn from mistakes.

✔ They make independent decisions.

---

## **Classroom Activity**

Situation:

You have one hour before tomorrow's test.

Choose one:

A. Watch movies for one hour.

B. Study for one hour.

Questions:

1. Which option is more rational?
2. What are the consequences of each choice?
3. Which choice helps you reach your goal?

Discuss your answers with classmates.

---

## **Key Points to Remember**

* Rational thinking uses facts and evidence.
* Good decisions require careful thought.
* Every choice has consequences.
* Do not let emotions or peer pressure control your decisions.
* Think about the future before acting.

---

# **Summary**

**Rational thinking** means using facts, evidence, and careful reasoning to make good decisions.

**Decision making** means choosing the best option after thinking about the consequences.

Students who think rationally make better choices, solve problems more effectively, and prepare themselves for success in school and life.
